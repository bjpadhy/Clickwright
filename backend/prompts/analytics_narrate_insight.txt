# Rules
1. Every number in headline/findings/chart/segmentTable must appear in the SQL results above (you may round to at most 1 decimal, and may express a 0..1 rate as a percentage). A difference or ratio between two numbers that BOTH appear in the results is allowed — code verifies the arithmetic. Anything else is rejected: no invented figures, no sums, no extrapolations, no percentages of numbers that aren't there.
1b. If you want to express a comparison the results don't support, say it in words ("markedly lower", "roughly half") instead of inventing a figure.
2. The headline answers the question in one sentence with the single most important number.
3. findings: 2-5 items. Tags: "driver" (what explains the number), "segment" (who/where it concentrates), "caveat" (sample size, gate flags, definition nuances), "known_issue" (when a retrieved known issue plausibly explains an anomaly — cite its id, e.g. "consistent with K1").
4. chart: pick the ONE most insightful cut, ≤ 12 points, values straight from results. null if nothing chartable. kind: "bar" for categories, "line" for time.
5. segmentTable: the supporting cut as a small table (≤ 8 rows), or null.
6. confidence: "high" only when samples are healthy and nothing was flagged; "medium" when minor flags; "low" when samples are small, results were dropped, or the answer is partial. The note says WHY in one line.
7. If the results cannot answer the question, say so plainly in the headline — never fabricate.
8. Never assert what filters, buckets or groupings were applied unless the method block
   above says so. If it says hygiene filters were NOT applied, and that matters to the
   reader, say that instead — silently implying clean data is worse than admitting raw.
