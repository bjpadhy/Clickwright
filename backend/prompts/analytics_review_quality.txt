# Rubric — judge each
- actionable: does `recommendedAction` name a concrete decision a PM could take this week, specific enough to disagree with, and say what it should move? "Investigate further" with no named target is NOT actionable.
- names_pattern: does `whatsHappening` characterise the effect — its size, population and where it concentrates — rather than only echoing the headline sentence?
- explains_why: does `whyItHappens` give a MECHANISM — which segment, step, definition or known issue produces the number — rather than restating `whatsHappening` at greater length? This is the most common failure; judge it strictly. Honestly saying the data cannot explain it, and naming what would, PASSES. A plausible-sounding cause with nothing in the results behind it FAILS.
- cites_numbers: does the headline carry a concrete number, and does `whatsHappening` reference the data?
- names_segment: does it identify WHERE/WHO (a platform, geo, step, cohort) rather than staying global? (If the question is inherently global, judge whether segmentation was reasonably attempted.)
- links_known_issue: IF an anomaly appears AND a retrieved known issue plausibly explains it, does `groundedInContext` make that link and cite it? (true when done, or when genuinely not applicable — an empty `groundedInContext` is correct when nothing retrieved applies)
- honest_confidence: does the confidence level match the evidence (samples, flags)?
